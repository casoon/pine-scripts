# Event types without a measurable entry edge

Measured broadly on CFDs across indices, commodities and currencies, on 1h, 4h and daily bars, with
costs and against random entries on the same side, the following event types gave **no robust net
edge as an entry signal**:

- oscillator line crosses and zero crosses
- oscillator extremes, also as "k of n oscillators in the zone"
- pivot divergences, regular and hidden
- structure breaks (break of structure, change of character)
- bounces off support and resistance zones, role reversal after a break
- false breakouts, liquidity sweeps of equal highs and lows
- chart formations (head and shoulders, rectangle, double bottom)
- breakout continuation after a new multi-week high
- pullbacks within a trend to simple price references
- short holding periods with tight stops on intraday timeframes

Some of them showed a small effect before costs or in one period. None held across periods and
instruments after costs.

## What this does and does not mean

- It does **not** mean these displays are useless. They describe where the market is, and that is
  what an indicator is for.
- It means labels, colours and texts should not attach a success probability to them. "Bullish
  divergence" is a description. "High-probability reversal" is a claim the data does not support.
- It covers the definitions that were measured. A clearly different definition is a new question.
- Volatility behaves differently from direction: it can be forecast. An ATR-based estimate of the
  expected range is useful for stop width and context, even though sizing positions by it did not
  improve results.
