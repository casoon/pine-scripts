# Win rate follows from geometry

The ratio of target to stop largely determines the win rate. A close target and a wide stop give a
high win rate whatever the signal is. A far target and a tight stop give a low one.

## What was seen

Entry signals whose win rate looked attractive turned out to sit exactly on the win rate of random
entries with the same target, stop and holding limit. The signal contributed nothing. The geometry
did all the work.

The same holds for a win-rate goal. A goal like "a high minimum share of winners" can be reached by moving the
target closer, without any change in the quality of the entries, and usually at the cost of the
average result per trade.

## What follows

- A win rate is only readable next to its **base rate**: the win rate of random entries with the same
  exit, on the same side, in the same period.
- The main measure for an entry is the net result per trade, compared with that base. The win rate
  describes the shape of the exit.
- Statistics panels that show a win rate without a base rate show the exit, not the signal.
